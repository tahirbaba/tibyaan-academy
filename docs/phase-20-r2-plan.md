# Plan: moving PDFs, images and video to Cloudflare R2

Plan only. No code has been written for this, and nothing here has been done.

## Why

Everything is on Supabase Storage today, on the free tier: **1 GB storage, 5 GB egress per
month**. Measured from the verified backup of 2026-09-11:

| Bucket | Objects | Size |
|---|---|---|
| `course-pdfs` | 60 | 644.8 MB |
| `videos` | 1 | 7.0 MB |
| `avatars` | 1 | 0.9 MB |
| **Total** | **62** | **652.7 MB — 65% of the limit** |

Two separate problems:

1. **Storage.** 645 MB of PDFs sit against a 1 GB cap before the video feature is used at all.
2. **Egress, which is the sharper one.** 5 GB/month. A 50 MB video watched 100 times consumes
   the entire month's allowance. The cost grows with every viewer, which is the opposite of
   what you want for a share link meant to spread.

R2 charges **$0.015/GB-month for storage and nothing for egress.** For a workload that is
mostly reads of large files, that is the whole argument.

## Cost comparison at 20 teachers × 10 videos

Using the one video on the platform (7 MB) as a floor — it is a sample of one and almost
certainly not a full tilawat recording — and 50 MB as a realistic figure.

| | Supabase free | Supabase Pro ($25/mo) | Cloudflare R2 |
|---|---|---|---|
| 200 × 7 MB = 1.4 GB + 645 MB PDFs | over the cap | included | **$0.03/mo** |
| 200 × 50 MB = 10 GB + 645 MB PDFs | over the cap | included | **$0.16/mo** |
| 200 × 150 MB = 30 GB + 645 MB PDFs | over the cap | over the 100 GB? no — included | **$0.46/mo** |
| Egress, 10 GB stored, 1,000 views/mo × 50 MB = 50 GB | over the cap | included to 250 GB | **$0.00** |
| Egress, as above but 5,000 views/mo = 250 GB | over the cap | at the limit | **$0.00** |

R2 also charges per operation: $4.50 per million writes, $0.36 per million reads. At this
scale that is cents. **Expected R2 bill at 20 teachers × 10 videos with moderate viewing:
well under $1/month**, against $25/month for Supabase Pro — and Pro still has an egress ceiling
that a viral share link can hit.

The saving is real but small in absolute terms. **The reason to move is the egress ceiling and
the cost curve, not the monthly figure.**

## What has to change

The storage layer is already isolated, which makes this smaller than it looks.
`src/lib/r2/client.ts` — named for R2, but currently a Supabase Storage client — is the only
module that talks to storage, and it exposes exactly eight functions:

```
getVideoKey  getThumbnailKey  getRecordingKey  getPublicUrl
getPresignedUploadUrl  getPresignedDownloadUrl  uploadToStorage  deleteFromStorage
```

Five files use them or hold storage URLs:

| File | What it holds |
|---|---|
| `src/lib/r2/client.ts` | the whole storage client |
| `src/app/api/videos/upload/route.ts` | presigned upload for teacher videos |
| `src/app/api/recordings/upload/route.ts` | presigned upload for class recordings |
| `src/app/[locale]/(auth)/signup/actions.ts` | avatar upload |
| `src/lib/data/course-syllabus.ts` | `PDF_BASE`, a hard-coded Supabase public URL |

R2 is S3-compatible, so `@aws-sdk/client-s3` and `@aws-sdk/s3-request-presigner` replace the
Supabase calls behind the same eight functions. **No caller changes** except
`course-syllabus.ts`, where one constant is swapped.

### Work, in order

1. **Create the R2 bucket and a public domain.** A custom domain such as
   `media.tibyaanacademy.com` — not the `r2.dev` development URL, which is rate-limited and
   not meant for production. New env vars: `R2_ACCOUNT_ID`, `R2_ACCESS_KEY_ID`,
   `R2_SECRET_ACCESS_KEY`, `R2_BUCKET`, `R2_PUBLIC_BASE_URL`.
2. **Reimplement the eight functions against R2**, keeping the signatures identical. Verify
   every existing caller still typechecks without modification.
3. **Copy the objects.** 62 objects, 653 MB — a single `rclone` or `aws s3 sync` run, minutes
   not hours. Copy, never move: Supabase keeps its copy until the cutover is proven.
4. **Switch reads.** `PDF_BASE` in `course-syllabus.ts` points at `R2_PUBLIC_BASE_URL`. Video
   and recording URLs are generated by `getPublicUrl`, so they follow automatically.
5. **Verify every URL before deleting anything.** A script that requests all 62 objects on R2
   and asserts 200 plus a matching content-length. This is the gate; nothing is deleted until
   it passes.
6. **Keep old links working.** Any `…supabase.co/storage/…` URL already shared must not break.
   Either leave the Supabase objects in place — 653 MB costs nothing extra while they fit the
   tier — or add a redirect route mapping the old path to the new one. **Leaving them is
   simpler and is what I would do**: they cost nothing and guarantee no share link ever 404s.
7. **Point new uploads at R2** and let Supabase Storage drain naturally.

### The images are not part of this

The 28 MB of syllabus images are now served from `public/`, which is the correct place for
them: they are small, they are versioned with the code, and they cost nothing on Vercel. They
were excluded from the deployment for R2 and then never moved, which is exactly how they came
to 404 for months. **Do not move them to R2** unless they grow by an order of magnitude.

## Risks

- **A share link that stops working is worse than the bill.** Step 6 is not optional.
- **Deleting the Supabase copies too early.** Step 5 gates step 7, and the constitution's
  migration rules apply: verify on a copy, keep a rollback.
- **R2's public bucket has no per-object access control.** Class recordings are private-ish
  today by obscurity (a long random key) and are auto-deleted after 30 days by the cleanup
  cron. If that is not good enough, recordings should use presigned URLs rather than a public
  bucket — worth deciding before the move, not after.
- **`r2.dev` in production.** It is rate-limited; the custom domain in step 1 matters.
